Section 1

Overview & Purpose

The Unified Market Intelligence Platform is PAMRA's central digital system. It integrates three previously fragmented systems — MCMIS, AMIS, and the Mandi Auction App — into a single source of truth, then verifies every record through a four-layer authenticity engine before publication.

Purpose

To eliminate data fragmentation, detect manipulation in real time, and give PAMRA leadership the trusted, integrated view needed for evidence-based agricultural policy in Punjab.

What the platform does

  • Integrates staff records (MCMIS), commodity prices (AMIS), and live auctions (Mandi App) into one canonical data model.
  • Captures data automatically at mandi gates via ANPR cameras, CNIC kiosks, weighbridges, and commodity classifiers — no manual entry.
  • Verifies every incoming record through four independent checks before publication.
  • Quarantines suspicious entries (price manipulation, ghost arrivals, geofence violations) for human review.
  • Surfaces live dashboards, alerts, and analytics for inspectors, committees and HQ.
  • Exports scheduled reports to leadership and regulatory bodies.

What end users see

Canonical record schema

Every record — regardless of source — is normalized to a single shape with required fields: record_id, source, market_id, commodity, lot_volume_kg, price_per_kg, auction_timestamp, staff_id, geo, external_ref. This makes cross-source comparison possible.

Section 3

Access & Sign in

Purpose

Restrict the platform to authorized PAMRA staff. Trader information is stored and used internally, but traders do not sign in — they are recognized at the gate by ANPR and CNIC scanning.

Who can sign in

  • PAMRA staff with an issued username and password (admin, inspector, analyst, committee secretary).
  • No public login for farmers or traders — they interact with PAMRA through public services (prices, market directory, helpline) and via physical gate operations.

How to sign in

  1. Click Platform Login in the top-right of any public PAMRA page (or visit /login).
  2. Enter your PAMRA-issued username and password.
  3. The system issues a 24-hour session token. You'll land on the portal showing only modules your role can access.
  4. Sign out from the top-right of any page to revoke your token immediately.

Forgot password?

Contact your PAMRA administrator. Passwords are managed centrally; there is no self-service reset.

Section 6

The Four-Layer Authenticity Engine

Purpose

Stop manipulation, errors, and fraud at the data layer — before bad records reach analytics, dashboards, and policy decisions. Each incoming record passes through four independent checks.

1

Cross-source validation

Compare the same auction's record as reported by MCMIS, AMIS, and Mandi App. If prices or volumes disagree by more than tolerance, the record is flagged.

Catches: Records that disagree across sources — typical sign of one source being tampered with.
2

Statistical anomaly detection

Maintain a 30-day rolling distribution per (market, commodity). Z-score on price (|z|>3 fails); IQR fence on volume. Also tracks entry velocity per staff member.

Catches: Impossible prices (10× normal), impossible volumes, fat-finger errors, fake bulk entry by single staff member.
3

Geo + staff verification

GPS coordinates of the record must lie within the market's stored geofence. The staff member's ID must appear on the day's biometric attendance roster.

Catches: Records submitted from outside the market premises; ghost entries under absent staff.
4

ML fraud scoring

A gradient-boosted classifier trained on historical disputed records. Features include price deviation, volume vs staff's history, time of day, entry velocity, and the staff member's prior dispute rate. Outputs a 0–1 confidence score.

Catches: Subtle patterns no single rule catches — e.g., a slightly off price, late at night, from a staff member with prior disputes.

Combined verdict

  • CLEAN → published to analytics and dashboards normally.
  • SUSPECT → published but visibly flagged; appears in review queue.
  • QUARANTINED → withheld from analytics; requires inspector/admin approval before release.

The threshold for quarantine is: any layer fails, OR fraud score > 0.70. Suspect: any layer warns, OR fraud score > 0.40.

Section 7

Gate Automation Flow

Purpose

Eliminate the gap between a truck's physical movement through the mandi and the digital record of that movement — automating capture so nothing is missed and nothing can be fabricated.

Each truck moves through 8 stages, with automatic capture at each station:

  1. APPROACHING — truck enters the queue. No active capture yet.
  2. ANPR_SCANNING — front camera reads the number plate. OCR confidence and registry match recorded.
  3. CNIC_KIOSK — driver presents CNIC at the kiosk. Verified against NADRA-style registry and against the plate-to-CNIC linkage.
  4. COMMODITY_SCAN — overhead camera classifies the produce in the truck bed; flagged if it doesn't match what was declared.
  5. WEIGHBRIDGE_IN — gross-in weight captured by load cell. Compared against declared volume.
  6. AUCTION — truck heads to the auction floor and unloads.
  7. WEIGHBRIDGE_OUT — tare-out weight after unloading. Net = gross − tare = produce actually sold.
  8. EXITED — a canonical arrival record is auto-generated and pushed into the authenticity engine.

What gets flagged at the gate

  • Plate not in the registered vehicles list → manual review required.
  • CNIC not in trader registry → driver verification needed.
  • CNIC and plate registered, but not to each other → identity mismatch.
  • Camera-classified commodity differs from what driver declared → physical inspection.
  • Gross weight differs >30% from declared load → re-weigh.
Section 8

Data Flow

Every record — whether it originates from a manual entry in MCMIS, a price publication in AMIS, a live auction in Mandi App, or a gate sensor — follows the same path:

  1. Capture — source system or gate sensor produces a raw record.
  2. Normalize — adapter maps the raw record into the canonical schema, attaches source tag.
  3. Publish — adapter writes the canonical record to the Data Bus.
  4. Verify — Authenticity Engine subscribes and runs all four layers on the record.
  5. Disposition — record is tagged CLEAN, SUSPECT, or QUARANTINED.
  6. Distribute — CLEAN goes to dashboards, analytics, reports; SUSPECT also distributed but visibly flagged; QUARANTINED held in review queue.
  7. Audit log — every step is recorded in a tamper-evident append-only log.
Section 9

Options & Configurable Settings

The platform exposes the following options for HQ administrators to tune its behaviour over time:

Authenticity engine

  • Cross-source tolerance — price disagreement % above which records warn (default 8%) or fail (default 20%).
  • Statistical window — rolling-window size for Z-score and IQR (default 30 days).
  • Fraud-score thresholds — boundaries for SUSPECT (default 0.40) and QUARANTINED (default 0.70) dispositions.
  • Per-commodity overrides — perishables get wider tolerance than staples.

Gate automation

  • Geofence radius per market (default 5 km).
  • ANPR confidence threshold below which manual review is triggered.
  • CNIC OCR + face match minimum confidence.
  • Weight variance threshold vs declared.

Reports

  • Schedule — daily / weekly / monthly cadence.
  • Recipients — email lists per report.
  • Format — PDF, XLSX, CSV.

Notifications

  • Alert channels — in-app, email, SMS to inspector on quarantine event.
  • Severity routing — high-severity infrastructure issues go to HQ directly.
Section 10

Operational Guidelines

For PAMRA HQ administrators

  • Review the quarantine queue at least once per business day. Records older than 72 hours without action should be escalated.
  • Audit user account access monthly; deactivate accounts of departed staff immediately.
  • Check adapter status weekly; any "degraded" status requires a manual resync.
  • Validate the fraud-score thresholds quarterly using the new month's disputed-records sample.

For market inspectors

  • Confirm gate cameras (ANPR + commodity overhead) are operational at start-of-day. Use the gate UI status indicator.
  • Review every QUARANTINED record from your market within 24 hours. Add a note when approving or rejecting.
  • If you receive a "staff not on duty" flag against your own ID, file an incident ticket — possible credential abuse.

For committee secretaries

  • Submit infrastructure issues immediately upon discovery — high-severity issues route to HQ within 1 hour.
  • Ensure biometric punch-in is functional; absent staff cannot legitimately submit records.
  • Review monthly finance reports before the 5th of each month and flag any discrepancies.

For HQ analysts

  • Use AMIS chart data for trend reporting — never use raw single-day prices for policy without 7-day smoothing.
  • When drafting a report based on quarantined records, always include the engine's reason field — it's part of the evidence trail.
  • Cross-check forecasts against arrivals data, not just published prices.
Section 11

Frequently Asked Questions

Why don't traders log in to the platform?

Traders are recognized at the mandi gate via their registered vehicle's number plate (ANPR) and their CNIC. There is no need for a separate trader login — PAMRA already has their information on file. This simplifies the system and reduces credential-management overhead for citizens. If a trader needs to query their auction history, they can request it via the helpline or in-person at any PAMRA office.

What happens to a quarantined record?

It is held out of analytics and dashboards. An inspector or admin must review it in the platform's quarantine queue and either approve (release into the data pool with a note) or reject (mark as confirmed bad and trigger a follow-up action like a show-cause notice). Every action is logged in the audit trail.

Can the engine produce false positives?

Yes, especially in early operation before the ML model has enough local data. SUSPECT dispositions are visible but published; QUARANTINED entries always require human review precisely so a false positive can be approved. Threshold tuning is part of monthly ops review.

What if the gate cameras fail?

An inspector falls back to manual entry via the legacy Mandi App. Records entered this way are tagged with source=MANDI_APP rather than GATE_AUTO and still pass through the engine, but they will not have the high-confidence "system-captured" provenance.

How does the platform protect citizen data?

All access is role-gated. CNIC numbers are only visible to admin role and at the gate kiosk during scan. The platform is hosted within the Punjab Government data centre with TLS in transit and at rest. Audit logs are append-only and tamper-evident.

Is the platform replacing MCMIS / AMIS / Mandi App?

No. They continue to operate as input systems. The platform sits above them as the canonical view and the verification layer. Over time, with HQ approval, the platform may become the primary system of record while the legacy ones become input UIs — this is Phase 3 of the rollout plan.

Section 12

Glossary

  • MCMIS — Market Committee Management Information System. PAMRA's existing system for staff/finance/infrastructure tracking.
  • AMIS — Agricultural Marketing Information System. PAMRA's existing system for publishing commodity prices.
  • Mandi App — Mobile/web app for inspectors to record live auctions at the mandi.
  • ANPR — Automatic Number Plate Recognition. Camera-based system that reads vehicle plates.
  • CNIC — Computerized National Identity Card. Pakistan's national ID.
  • NADRA — National Database and Registration Authority. Pakistan's identity authority.
  • Mandi — Pakistani term for a wholesale agricultural produce market or auction yard.
  • Geofence — A virtual perimeter around a real-world location. Used to confirm a record originates inside a market.
  • Quarantine — A record withheld from analytics pending human review.
  • Z-score — Number of standard deviations a value is from the mean. Z-score >3 means very unusual.
  • IQR — Interquartile range. Used as a fence for volume outliers.
  • Tare / Gross / Net — Empty truck / loaded truck / cargo weight. Net = gross − tare.
